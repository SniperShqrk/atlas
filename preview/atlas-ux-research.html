What ATLAS should steal, and what it should refuse

Six findings from the competitive field and the mobile-subscription data, each with the decision it forces. Principles and evidence only — no borrowed layouts, no borrowed copy.

September 2026 Inputs: Hevy · Strong · Fitbod · subscription benchmarks

The gap ATLAS sits in

The two apps serious lifters actually use split the problem cleanly, and neither side picks up what the other drops. That split is the opening.

Hevy
Logging is fast and the interface is clean. Analytics are thin.
Strong
Deeper analytics and more graph types. Steeper to learn, more to hold.
ATLAS
Hevy's logging speed, analytics past Strong's, and the one thing neither ships: a reading of what the numbers mean.

Both competitors present data and stop. Charts are the raw material of an answer, not the answer. ATLAS's insight engine is the differentiator, so every other decision below is judged by whether it gets a user to the point where that engine has something to say.

Six findings

1. Speed of logging is the whole product

The most consistent thing lifters ask for is minimal tapping between sets. Everything else is negotiable; this is not. An app that is slow mid-session gets abandoned mid-session.

Users value apps that minimise "time tapping between sets." Hevy's stated strength is exactly this: "Add a set, record reps and weight, and move on." Setgraph, Reddit gym-app synthesis · Setgraph, Hevy vs Strong
Decision

The workout screen is frozen as a speed surface. Nothing decorative, nothing informational, nothing that costs a tap gets added to it — the marble textures and statue artwork in the visual identity work go everywhere except here. This retroactively justifies stripping it to monochrome, and it becomes a standing rule rather than a one-off fix.

2. The first fourteen days decide everything

Churn is not spread evenly across the life of an account. It is concentrated almost entirely at the start, and the threshold is low enough to design against directly.

Users who complete fewer than three workouts in their first 14 days churn at 3–4× the rate of those who establish a weekly habit. The top cancellation reason across fitness subscriptions is loss of motivation, at 38%. RetentionCheck, fitness app churn benchmarks 2026
Decision

ATLAS gets an explicit first-fortnight goal: get the user to three logged sessions. That is a concrete, measurable design target, not a vague onboarding improvement — a visible "3 sessions to your first reading" marker, and a home screen that states plainly what unlocks at three.

3. The cold start is where AI fitness apps die

This is the most important finding for ATLAS specifically, because it describes a failure mode the app currently walks straight into.

On Fitbod, users "probably decide it's not worth it before the algorithm has enough data" — the value only becomes clear after 4+ weeks of consistent use, by which point a monthly subscriber has already cancelled. Fitness Tools Reviewed, Fitbod review
Decision

ATLAS currently shows nothing until three sessions are logged (sessionsUntilInsights), which is the same trap. The fix is that the empty state must itself demonstrate the product: show a real worked example on sample data, clearly labelled as an example, so a day-one user sees exactly what ATLAS will say about them. The insight engine is deterministic, so this costs nothing to generate and cannot misrepresent what the real thing does.

4. Free-tier caps are the most-hated pattern, and soft paywalls convert better anyway

The instinct to restrict logging is both the thing users complain about most and, on the numbers, the weaker commercial choice for an app whose pitch is analysis.

Paywall complaints across the field are about capped functionality on the free tier. Meanwhile soft paywalls convert roughly 50% better than hard ones, while hard paywalls produce 21% higher LTV — the trade only favours hard walls when the product is valuable on day one. Setgraph, Reddit synthesis · Adapty, high-performing paywalls 2026
Decision

Hold the existing line — logging, history, the recovery map and the exercise library stay uncapped forever. ATLAS is not valuable on day one by construction (finding 3), so the hard-paywall LTV premium is not available to us regardless. The soft wall is both the honest choice and the correct one.

5. Purchases happen on day zero, which fights our own product

The subscription data is unambiguous about timing, and it points in the opposite direction from finding 3. This tension is the central design problem of ATLAS's monetisation.

44.5% of all purchases happen on Day 0 and 90% of trial starts do too. Onboarding paywalls convert at 1.35% versus 0.89% in-app. In Health & Fitness specifically, trials increase LTV, and a trial on the annual plan is the structure top apps converge on. Adapty, high-performing paywalls 2026
Decision

Two shots, not one. An onboarding paywall that sells the promise concretely — the worked example from finding 3, not a feature list — with a trial on the annual plan. Then the contextual moment already built: the first real "ATLAS found 3 things" card, which lands around session three and is the honest, earned pitch. The Day-0 attempt captures the buyers who decide fast; the in-app moment captures the ones who need proof.

6. ATLAS is priced too low, and the lifetime tier is cannibalising it

The current price list was set by intuition. The benchmarks say intuition was wrong in a specific and correctable direction.

In Health & Fitness, expensive annual plans generate 4.5× more LTV, and nine in ten subscriptions sell at full price. Price increases win 45.5% of LTV tests. Fitbod charges £12.99/month or £79.99/year. Adapty · Fitness Tools Reviewed

What this means for the visual identity work

Item 4 is next, and the research constrains it more than it liberates it. Three rules carried forward:

  • Texture goes on the reading surfaces, never the working ones. Home, Progress, Profile, milestones and the paywall get marble, stone and statuary. The workout logger and the exercise library stay flat, fast and quiet.
  • Themes must not change information design. Marble, Obsidian, Bronze and Stone swap tokens only. Terracotta means fatigued or neglected in every theme; bronze means recovering, complete, or Premium in every theme. A theme that re-teaches the colour language is a bug.
  • Legibility is the acceptance test. A light Marble theme has to hold the same contrast on the recovery heatmap and the balance radar as the dark ones. If a theme cannot carry the data, it does not ship.

Three decisions I need from you

Pricing

Raise the tiers as proposed, and remove or reprice lifetime? This is the single highest- leverage change on the list and it costs nothing to implement.

My recommendation: raise, and remove lifetime entirely before launch.

Onboarding paywall

Day-0 paywall with a worked example and a 7-day trial on annual, or stay purely contextual and let the insight moment do all the selling?

My recommendation: both, as described in finding 5.

Social features

Challenges, leaderboards and friend connections correlate with 20–35% lower monthly churn, but they are a large build and they pull ATLAS toward being a feed.

My recommendation: skip for v1. Shareable progress cards get most of the reach without any of the social infrastructure — and they double as the marketing asset in item 10.
